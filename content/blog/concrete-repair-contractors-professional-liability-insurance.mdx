One cracked slab can turn a routine concrete repair project into a costly insurance claim. Is your concrete repair business properly protected?

Concrete repair contractors face unique risks every day. Whether you're performing concrete lifting, polyurethane foam injection, mudjacking, slab stabilization, foundation repair, or void filling, you're working with existing structures that may already have hidden weaknesses.

What happens when a concrete slab cracks, breaks, shifts, or collapses during your work?

Many contractors assume their General Liability insurance will automatically cover the damage. Unfortunately, that's not always the case.

Understanding the difference between General Liability and Contractors Professional Liability insurance could save your concrete repair business thousands of dollars in unexpected expenses.

## The Hidden Risks of Concrete Lifting and Repair

Imagine your company is hired to lift a sunken driveway using polyurethane foam injection.

Your technician drills injection holes, positions the equipment, and begins pumping material beneath the concrete. During the lifting process, excessive pressure or uneven movement causes the slab to crack completely.

The homeowner now wants the entire damaged section removed and replaced.

Who pays for the damage?

The answer depends on your insurance policy, the cause of the damage, and whether the claim involves faulty workmanship, physical property damage, or a professional error.

Common situations that can lead to expensive claims include:

- **Slab cracking during lifting** — injection pressure or uneven lifting causes an existing concrete slab to fracture
- **Excessive lifting** — a technician raises a slab too high, damaging adjacent concrete, garage floors, steps, or foundations
- **Incorrect soil or void assessment** — a contractor misjudges subsurface conditions, resulting in additional settlement or structural movement
- **Foundation damage** — stabilization or lifting operations contribute to cracking in a foundation, wall, or other structural component
- **Drainage problems** — an incorrectly designed repair changes water flow and causes additional property damage
- **Improper repair recommendations** — a contractor recommends a repair method that fails to address the underlying structural problem

Even experienced contractors can encounter these situations.

## Why General Liability Insurance May Not Cover a Broken Slab

Commercial General Liability (CGL) insurance is essential for concrete repair contractors. It generally provides coverage for covered third-party bodily injury and property damage claims.

However, most General Liability policies contain exclusions that may affect claims involving damage to the contractor's own work. Two particularly important exclusions are commonly referred to as:

## 1. The "Your Work" Exclusion

This exclusion can restrict coverage for damage to the contractor's completed work arising from that work, subject to policy terms and exceptions.

## 2. The "That Particular Part" Exclusions

These exclusions may restrict coverage for damage to the particular portion of property on which the contractor is performing operations or which must be repaired because the contractor's work was incorrectly performed.

For example, if a concrete lifting contractor damages the exact slab being lifted, the insurance carrier may determine that some or all of the repair costs are excluded.

Damage to surrounding property may be treated differently.

Important: Not every cracked slab is excluded from General Liability coverage. The outcome depends on the policy wording, endorsements, cause of loss, timing of the damage, and applicable law.

## How Contractors Professional Liability Insurance Can Help

Contractors Professional Liability insurance, sometimes called Contractors Errors and Omissions (E&O) insurance, can help protect contractors against certain claims arising from negligent professional services.

Depending on the policy, covered professional services may include:

- Concrete repair consulting and recommendations
- Repair design and specifications
- Structural evaluation or assessment
- Professional project management
- Engineering-related services performed within the contractor's authorized scope
- Other specifically defined professional services

For example, suppose a concrete stabilization contractor evaluates a settling slab and recommends a repair procedure. The recommendation is negligently developed, and the resulting repair causes additional structural damage.

If the contractor's policy covers that type of professional service and resulting loss, Professional Liability coverage may respond.

However, there is an important distinction:

**Professional Liability insurance does not automatically cover a concrete slab that breaks during ordinary lifting or repair operations.**

Many professional liability policies exclude faulty workmanship, damage to the contractor's own work, or physical construction activities unless coverage is specifically endorsed.

This makes selecting the correct insurance program especially important.

## What Insurance Should Cover a Slab That Breaks During Lifting?

Concrete lifting and repair contractors should discuss several coverage options with an insurance specialist.

| Insurance Coverage | Potential Protection |
|---|---|
| Commercial General Liability | Covered third-party bodily injury and property damage, subject to exclusions |
| Contractors Professional Liability | Covered negligent professional services, design errors, or professional recommendations |
| Contractors Errors & Omissions / Faulty Workmanship Coverage | Certain costs arising from defective work, when expressly included |
| Rectification or Mitigation Coverage | Certain costs to correct qualifying errors before they lead to larger covered losses |
| Contractors Pollution Liability | Certain pollution-related incidents involving chemicals or materials, if covered |

Not every policy includes these protections. Some specialty programs may combine multiple coverages, while others require separate policies or endorsements.

For contractors performing polyurethane concrete lifting, mudjacking, or slab stabilization, specific coverage for accidental damage to the slab being worked on may be more relevant than a standard Professional Liability policy alone.

## Real-World Example: A $25,000 Concrete Repair Claim

Consider this hypothetical situation.

A concrete lifting contractor is hired to raise a settled commercial warehouse floor.

During the injection process, a section of the slab fractures. The building owner demands replacement of the damaged concrete and compensation for interrupted operations.

The contractor faces the following expenses:

- Concrete demolition and removal: $5,000
- Replacement concrete and labor: $12,000
- Equipment and cleanup: $3,000
- Business interruption demand: $5,000

**Total potential claim: $25,000**

The contractor submits the claim to its General Liability carrier.

The carrier reviews whether the damaged slab falls within an applicable exclusion relating to the particular part of property being worked on.

If coverage is denied, the contractor may have to pay the expenses out of pocket.

A properly structured specialty insurance program might provide some protection, depending on its specific coverage grants, exclusions, limits, and deductibles.

The critical issue is having the right coverage before the damage occurs.

## Five Questions Every Concrete Repair Contractor Should Ask Their Insurance Agent

Before starting another lifting or stabilization project, ask:

1. Does my General Liability policy cover damage to the concrete slab I am actively lifting or repairing?
2. Does my policy contain exclusions for concrete lifting, mudjacking, polyurethane injection, foundation stabilization, or structural repair?
3. Would Contractors Professional Liability cover an incorrect repair recommendation or professional assessment?
4. Is there an endorsement available for faulty workmanship, resulting damage, or accidental damage to property being worked on?
5. Are my actual operations accurately described and accepted by the insurance carrier?

Do not rely on verbal assurances alone. Ask your insurance agent to identify the relevant policy provisions and obtain written clarification from the carrier when necessary.

## Why Specialized Concrete Repair Insurance Matters

Concrete lifting and stabilization are not the same as ordinary concrete flatwork.

These contractors frequently work on existing structures, using specialized equipment and materials to change the elevation, support, or stability of concrete.

Insurance companies may classify these operations differently, and some carriers may restrict or exclude certain methods.

A contractor performing polyurethane foam injection beneath a foundation has a different risk profile than a contractor pouring new sidewalks.

Your insurance program should reflect the work you actually perform.
